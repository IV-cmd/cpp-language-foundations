The first matching catch wins
Handler (in order)throw std::out_of_range(…)
catch (const std::out_of_range& e)matches runs this one
catch (const std::exception& e)skipped
out_of_range derives from std::exception, so the second handler would also match. Handlers are tried top to bottom, so the specific ones go first. In the reverse order, GCC warns that the specific handler will never be reached.
